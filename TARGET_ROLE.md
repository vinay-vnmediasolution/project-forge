# Target Role

**Purpose:** Build toward Forward Deployed Engineer–grade capability — the
intersection of production engineering, applied AI, and customer-facing
delivery. This file defines what the market screens for, so the programme is
driven by requirements rather than assumption.

This file is public deliberately. The scores below are honest rather than
flattering, and they are expected to move in both directions.

---

## What the role is

**Titles:** Forward Deployed Engineer / Forward Deployed Software Engineer.
The same work is hired under plainer names: Applied AI Engineer, AI Solutions
Engineer, Customer/Deployment Engineer, Technical Deployment Lead, Solutions
Architect (AI).

**The shape of it:** embed with a customer, understand the real problem
quickly, design and build the solution, deploy it, and prove it created value.
Palantir describes the responsibilities as resembling those of a startup CTO.

Every capability on this list compounds into independent consulting delivery.
Nothing here is single-purpose.

## Consolidated requirements

Synthesised from Palantir, OpenAI, Google Cloud, Databricks, Salesforce,
Deloitte and Accenture postings.

### R1 — Production engineering experience
5+ years engineering or technical deployment experience, typically
customer-facing, with the ability to write **and review** production-grade code
across backend and frontend. Palantir specifies strong coding proficiency in
Python, Java, C++ or TypeScript.

### R2 — LLM and agentic systems fluency
Hands-on experience building or deploying LLM-powered systems in production or
client environments — understanding how model behaviour affects the product
experience, not theoretical familiarity. Deloitte's Palantir practice asks for
1+ years of shipped GenAI/LLM solutions specifically.

### R3 — Evaluation-driven thinking
Systematic evaluation using representative data, production signals and human judgement. Published accounts of frontier-lab hiring have described evals as non-negotiable; that is an attributed characterisation, not a claim that every FDE posting uses those words. Current OpenAI deployment roles explicitly mention evals, error analysis and tightening success criteria over time.

### R4 — Data engineering at scale
ETL/ELT pipelines, warehousing, large-scale integration. Spark, Airflow and dbt
recur. Palantir emphasises wrangling large-scale messy data. Data modelling
listed as core.

### R5 — Full-stack breadth
Debug across data, backend and application layers. Google Cloud's version asks
for deep enough understanding of distributed systems and API design to spot
problems. Docker, CI/CD, cloud.

### R6 — Rapid prototyping under ambiguity
Prototype from a business requirement, iterate on real-world feedback, build
from scratch when needed. Palantir describes projects starting from open-ended
questions. Explicitly: strong problem-solving in ambiguous environments.

### R7 — Customer-facing ownership
Lead technical discussions with stakeholders from engineers to executives;
translate business priorities into delivery. Gather requirements, analyse
workflows, define a roadmap from prototype to scaled deployment.

### R8 — Regulated-environment delivery
Demand concentrates in financial services, government and healthcare, where
compliance sits alongside technical integration.

### R9 — Enterprise systems literacy
Familiarity with enterprise or operational systems — ERPs, CRMs, ITSM, GIS.

### R10 — Disposition
Radical ownership, customer empathy, problem decomposition, product sense,
willingness to travel (typically ~25%). Interviews are reported to test
deployment thinking under ambiguity rather than algorithmic problem solving.

## Gap scoring

Scored personally, not by a model. Against a hiring panel's standard, not a
peer group's.

- **1** — no evidence I could show anyone
- **2** — conceptual understanding, nothing built
- **3** — built it once, could not defend it under questioning
- **4** — shipped it, can explain the trade-offs, have been wrong and corrected
- **5** — could teach it, and a third party has relied on it

## Coach's provisional read

Recorded to be argued with, not accepted. The disagreements against the
self-scores are the useful part.

**Likely strong (4+):** R7, R8, R9. Enterprise stakeholder work, regulated
delivery and enterprise systems literacy are hard to fake and scarce in this
candidate pool.

**Likely mid (3):** R1, R6. Real delivery experience, but production code
authorship is where a panel will press.

**Likely weak (1–2):** R2, R3, R4. **R3 is the highest-leverage gap** — most
demanded, least commonly held.
